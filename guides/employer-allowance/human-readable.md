# Designing an employer allowance

Version 1.2.0 · Reviewed November 28, 2026 · U.S. federal framework

An employer allowance is a way to decide which costs the plan helps employees pay and how much choice employees retain. Start with what the employer wants to make affordable, what employees can purchase through the plan, and where unrestricted compensation would serve them better. The group health plan can combine insurance, direct care and other eligible benefits. An employer can change one component while keeping the funding rules and records it owns. ## Design the allowance

1. **Start with a participant outcome.** Define the employer's budget, the target employee premium, and the forms of care or coverage the credit should help people afford. Compare representative employees by class, age or household where relevant. Show their premium after the credit, the amount still available for other permitted uses, and any coverage or financial exposure they retain.
2. **Give each amount a job.** A direct contribution helps pay for one benefit. A plan-wide flex credit lets employees choose among the benefits and expenses named in the plan. A separate taxable amount can give employees broader control. Document what employees give up when they choose a different benefit, enroll, waive coverage, or leave part of a credit unused. Test the coverage and affordability effects before deciding how much money to restrict.
3. **Make the tradeoff visible.** For example, a $270 monthly employee premium and a $220 target call for $50 in eligible funding. A separate $100 cashable cafeteria credit preserves more options for that $100, while only the $50 qualifying health credit enters the affordability reduction. In the participant's explanation show where the $50 can go, how the $100 can be used, and whether any conditions apply. Use the same disclosed cost and coverage criteria when comparing carriers, care providers or administrators. Include relevant fees and gaps in coverage when comparing independent alternatives.
4. **Make the terms transferable.** Keep dated plan terms, calculation inputs, offers, elections and payroll records under the sponsor's control. Assign stable identifiers to funding amounts and coverage components. An administrator can run elections, a payroll provider can show where the money went, and an advisor can review the legal determination using the same terms. Give each party access to the evidence it needs for its role. ## Federal compliance

Start with the adopted rights for each employer-funded amount. A split allowance can appear as one enrollment budget. Its terms, elections, payroll, claims and affordability records should identify each amount independently. Collect the plan year, funding source, applicable employee class, coverage or expense uses, taxable election rights, cash conditions, waiver terms and the evidence that administration follows the adopted terms. [CommonFunds, “The four allowance tranches”](../../plan-structures/common-funds/human-readable.md#the-four-allowance-tranches)

### Health flex, general flex and direct funding

- A cafeteria-plan amount can reduce the required contribution as **health flex** only if the employee cannot elect cash or another taxable benefit, can use the amount for minimum essential coverage (MEC), and can spend it exclusively on §213 medical care. Document the eligible premium and the amount made available for the current plan year. For §4980H(b) and Form 1095-C, spread a qualifying contribution ratably over the period it covers. A stand-alone FSA-only amount fails the MEC-use condition. [CommonDocs CHOICE / ICHRA, “Health-flex credit”](../../plan-structures/choice-ichra/human-readable.md) · [26 CFR §1.36B-2(c)(3)(v)(A)(6)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.36B-2) · [IRS Notice 2015-87, Q&A 8](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2015-52_IRB)
- **General flex** can fund designated cafeteria benefits or taxable wages under the plan terms. The amount receives zero health-flex reduction if cash, another taxable option or nonmedical spending is permitted. Conditional cash eligibility still creates a potential cash right. For a split design, establish the protected health-only tranche as a separate right. A cash threshold or cap alone does not identify which dollars have that protection. A cashable amount elected into a Health FSA counts as salary reduction. For excepted-benefit status, verify availability of other qualifying group coverage and annual maximum FSA benefit of at most `max(2 × S, S + $500)`, where `S` includes cashable amounts directed into the FSA. Check the applicable annual §125(i) salary-reduction limit separately. [CommonFunds, “The four allowance tranches” and “Apply the cash-unlock condition”](../../plan-structures/common-funds/human-readable.md) · [IRS Notice 2012-40](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-12-40.pdf) · [45 CFR §146.145(b)(3)(v)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-146.145)
- A **direct contribution** can lower the employee premium for the associated employer offer. If using a quoted employee premium that already reflects it, enter that net premium once. Keep distinct ICHRA, EBHRA, other HRA, FSA and HSA vehicle rules. Apply the applicable vehicle limits and tests before counting an amount toward affordability. For an integrated group HRA reduction, verify that the same employer offers both arrangements, the new HRA dollars can pay premiums, and the annual amount is required or timely determinable under the applicable rule. [26 CFR §1.36B-2(c)(3)(v)(A)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.36B-2)

### Cafeteria-plan nondiscrimination

**A class-based contribution can be permissible.** A written plan could give a defined class of lower-paid hourly employees $200 per month for eligible premiums and give salaried employees $0, while letting both groups pay their share pre-tax. Test who actually qualifies for the credit and what each group can elect under the full plan. If the salaried group also loses access to pre-tax elections, that changes the eligibility test. Giving the new credit only to non-highly compensated employees does not establish a pass for the rest of the plan. [26 USC §125(b)–(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s125/) · [Proposed Treasury Regulation §1.125-7(b)–(c)](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB)

Section 125 sets the requirements. The IRS permits reliance on the 2007 proposed regulations for the detailed testing method pending final regulations. [IRS 2007 proposed regulations, “Proposed Effective Date”](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB)

| Test | What the plan administrator checks |
| --- | --- |
| **Eligibility** | Does access favor highly compensated individuals? Use a reasonable classification based on objective criteria, such as job category or location. Under the proposed method, divide the percentage of non-highly compensated employees benefiting by the percentage of highly compensated employees benefiting and compare with the applicable safe-harbor percentage for the workforce. A result below the safe harbor can require a facts-and-circumstances analysis, subject to the applicable minimum ratio. [Proposed §1.125-7(b)](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB) · [26 CFR §1.410(b)-4](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.410(b)-4) · [26 CFR §1.410(b)-9](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.410(b)-9) |
| **Contributions and benefits** | Do the terms or actual elections favor highly compensated participants? Check that similarly situated participants have a uniform opportunity to elect benefits and use employer funding. Under the proposed utilization test, compare each group's aggregate qualified benefits elected, or applicable employer contributions used for qualified benefits, as a percentage of that group's aggregate compensation. For example, 8% for highly compensated participants versus 5% for other participants is disproportionate under the general proposed test. [26 USC §125(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s125/) · [Proposed §1.125-7(c), (k)](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB) |
| **Key-employee concentration** | Divide qualified benefits provided to key employees by qualified benefits provided to all employees under the cafeteria plan. More than 25% fails. Include the applicable benefits across the plan even when a key employee gets $0 of the new contribution. Identify key employees separately under §416(i). [26 USC §125(b)(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s125/) · [Proposed §1.125-7(d)](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB) |

Identify highly compensated individuals using the applicable officer, ownership, compensation and family rules. Apply the relevant controlled-group rules. Under the proposed method, test as of the last day of the plan year using the applicable non-excludable population, including people who worked during the year and then left. Retain the census, class criteria, elections, compensation and benefit totals with the result. A failed test generally affects the tax treatment of the highly compensated participants or key employees covered by that test. [26 USC §125(b), (e), (g)(4)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s125/) · [Proposed §1.125-7(a), (i), (j), (m)](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB)

**Check the applicable alternative and benefit-specific rules.** Under §125(j), an eligible small employer must satisfy the simple plan's eligibility rules and fund each qualified employee. The contribution is either a uniform rate of at least 2% of compensation or at least the lesser of 6% of compensation and twice the employee's salary reduction, subject to the matching-rate restriction. The §125(g)(2) major-medical safe harbor covers contributions and benefits. For each similarly situated participant, the baseline contribution must meet either the 100% cost benchmark for coverage of the majority of similarly situated highly compensated participants or at least 75% of the highest-cost similarly situated participant's coverage. Amounts above the applicable baseline must bear a uniform relationship to compensation. The proposed premium-only safe harbor is limited to plans that meet its definition and eligibility test. For a Health FSA, the proposed definition of employer flex credits calls for availability to every cafeteria-plan-eligible employee, and §105(h) applies separately where applicable. Verify the exact scope of any safe harbor before relying on it. [26 USC §125(g), (j)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s125/) · [Proposed §§1.125-5(b), 1.125-7(e)–(f)](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2007-39_IRB)

**Checker inputs.** Supply verified plan-year eligibility and contributions-and-benefits results in `cafeteria.eligibilityTestPass` and `cafeteria.contributionsTestPass`. The checker reads those determinations and calculates key-employee concentration from verified totals. Leave an undetermined field absent or null. Set `cafeteria.nondiscriminationExceptionVerified` to true only when the evidence establishes relief covering all three checks. For relief limited to eligibility or contributions and benefits, document it in the corresponding determination and complete the other checks.

### Cash wages, waiver payments and private alternatives

Unconditional taxable compensation can preserve employee choice. Document ordinary payroll treatment, freedom to spend, and independence from purchase, enrollment and proof of coverage. A waiver payment has a different trigger: declining the offer. It can **increase** required contribution. For §4980H and reporting, Notice 2015-87 addresses unconditional waiver arrangements adopted after December 16, 2015 and transitional treatment. Keep adoption history, payment amount, waiver trigger, other coverage and proof requirements. Apply a source- and period-specific determination for conditional arrangements or claimed relief. The 2016 opt-out proposal should be recorded as proposed authority. [IRS Notice 2015-17, Q&A 4](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2015-14_IRB#NOT-2015-17) · [IRS Notice 2015-87, Q&A 9](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2015-52_IRB) · [2016 proposed opt-out rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2016-07-08/pdf/2016-15940.pdf) · [IRS Instructions for Forms 1094-C and 1095-C, Line 15](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i109495c)

For the Alternative Companion or PTC Plan design, the participant can use ordinary wages after payment to buy an independent option. Keep any neutral, voluntary post-tax payroll remittance separate from employer premium funding. A premium reimbursement or cash conditioned on buying individual-market insurance triggers employer-arrangement analysis even if taxed. Direct individual-premium funding requires an applicable compliant vehicle, such as a properly integrated ICHRA. Section 125 generally bars Exchange QHP purchases with the statutory qualified-employer Exchange exception. A §125 election for the unreimbursed portion of an eligible **off-Exchange** individual premium alongside an ICHRA is a separate path. [Alternative Companion, “Payroll and employer allowances”](../../plan-structures/alternative-companion/human-readable.md) · [PTC Plan, “Unrestricted wages versus an employer payment plan”](../../plan-structures/ptc-plan/human-readable.md) · [DOL ACA FAQ Part XXII, Q1](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/faqs/aca-part-22) · [26 USC §125(f)(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-118/t26/s125/) · [Final ICHRA rule, salary-reduction arrangements](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2019-28_IRB)

For an insured voluntary program relying on the ERISA safe harbor, verify no employer contribution, completely voluntary participation, limited neutral employer functions without endorsement, and no employer consideration beyond permitted reasonable administrative compensation. For health sharing and other non-insurance alternatives, review establishment or maintenance under ERISA based on actual conduct. Review actual enrollment, communications, payroll and funding practices. [Alternative Companion, “Shared guide or portal” discussion](../../plan-structures/alternative-companion/human-readable.md) · [29 CFR §2510.3-1(j)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-2510.3-1) · [29 USC §1002(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t29/s1002/)

### ICHRA class design and affordability

**Design schedule.** Document how each enrollment or payroll class maps to a permitted regulatory class and verify membership for the plan year. Establish the class definitions before the year. Verify traditional group offers by class and no dual offer of traditional coverage and ICHRA to the same class, subject to the special new-hire rule. Allowed classes include full-time, part-time, salaried, nonsalaried, applicable primary worksite rating-area, seasonal, certain collectively bargained, waiting-period, qualifying nonresident alien and temporary-placement classes, and permitted combinations. Class size applies only in the mixed traditional-group/ICHRA offer situations specified in (d)(3), with exceptions including statewide geography, specified full-time/part-time patterns, and waiting-period combinations. When applicable, compare employees offered the ICHRA on day one against a minimum of 10 for employers below 100, floor of 10% for 100–200, and 20 above 200. Use the employer's reasonable advance estimate of day-one employment for the denominator. [CHOICE / ICHRA, “ICHRA-only employers and the minimum-class-size rule”](../../plan-structures/choice-ichra/human-readable.md) · [26 CFR §54.9802-4(c)(2), (d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-54.9802-4)

**Same terms.** Test the maximum offered dollars on comparable terms within each class. Permitted variations include age, increasing amounts for more covered dependents with equal amounts for the same dependent count on comparable terms, and permitted uniform proration. For age variation, apply the same age determination method and applicable same-age consistency and test the 3:1 oldest-to-youngest maximum under (c)(3)(iii)(B). Compare authoritatively normalized schedule rows and preserve facts about eligible participants. Compensation or actual selected premium is an impermissible basis for varying the maximum available within a class. Reimbursing actual eligible expenses up to a compliant maximum is a separate operation. A benchmark may inform the design schedule. A rating-area benchmark or premium ratio does not by itself prove same terms. [CHOICE / ICHRA, class and benchmark examples](../../plan-structures/choice-ichra/human-readable.md) · [26 CFR §54.9802-4(c)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-54.9802-4) · [2019 final rule, same-terms discussion](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2019-28_IRB)

**Administration.** Verify qualifying individual insurance or applicable Medicare integration, annual and ongoing substantiation, opt-out and waiver procedures, and a timely written notice with the actual maximum and self-only amount. Notice is generally at least 90 days before the plan year, with eligibility and timing exceptions in (c)(6). Verify applicable §105(h) and other plan requirements independently. Verify the actual adopted ICHRA maximum even when a larger flex allowance appears alongside it. [CHOICE / ICHRA, ICHRA amount and participant notice](../../plan-structures/choice-ichra/human-readable.md) · [26 CFR §54.9802-4(c)(1), (4)–(6), (e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-54.9802-4) · [Proposed ICHRA rule, §105(h) and reliance terms](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2019-20034)

**Affordability benchmark.** The federal §36B ICHRA basis is the excess of the applicable monthly self-only Exchange **lowest-cost silver plan** premium at the employee's residence over the self-only HRA dollars newly available for the plan year divided by available months. If the HRA has a single maximum regardless of coverage tier, use that maximum. The new amount must be determinable within a reasonable time before the plan year. Exclude carryover and amounts attributable to balances under a prior arrangement. Test against the §36B monthly household-income threshold, subject to applicable rules for partial year, Exchange determinations and opt-out or enrollment. The second-lowest-cost silver benchmark figures in the PTC amount, a separate calculation. For §4980H use the documented employer method, including only applicable safe harbors. The ICHRA employer location, look-back month and age methods are addressed in proposed rules with reliance terms. Use the premium basis specified for the legal affordability test when the design schedule was built using a different benchmark. [CHOICE / ICHRA, “LCSP” and “ALE affordability”](../../plan-structures/choice-ichra/human-readable.md) · [26 CFR §1.36B-2(c)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.36B-2) · [Proposed §54.4980H-5(f) and reliance](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2019-20034) · [IRS Form 8962 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8962)

**Separate ICHRA and health-flex dollars.** The CHOICE design example describes subtracting a separately qualifying health-flex amount from the ICHRA baseline where the participant can use it for eligible unreimbursed off-Exchange MEC. This combines the general cafeteria rule with the special ICHRA rule. Treat that second subtraction as an interpretive scenario requiring authority and reviewer approval for the intended use. The Form 1095-C instructions generally describe the ICHRA amount as LCSP minus ICHRA funding. Preserve and show the regulatory baseline and any approved alternative separately. [CHOICE / ICHRA, “The operative affordability rule”](../../plan-structures/choice-ichra/human-readable.md) · [26 CFR §1.36B-2(c)(3)(v)(A)(6), (c)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.36B-2) · [IRS Instructions for Forms 1094-C and 1095-C, Line 15](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i109495c)

## Run the standalone checker

The local reference checker evaluates a dated allowance revision for one plan, subject or verified cohort, and purpose. It has no enrollment or payroll connection. It uses a public JSON contract, local code and evidence references supplied by the plan. A different administrator or agent can use the same files and reproduce the findings.

| File | Use |
| --- | --- |
| [Assessment input schema](assessment-input.schema.json) | Draft 2020-12 JSON Schema for proposal, legal rights, assessment facts and evidence references. |
| [Assessment result schema](assessment-result.schema.json) | Stable output contract for findings, exact amounts and assessment status. |
| [Reference implementation](check_allowance.py) | Validates input, evaluates the covered rules and validates its output. Run with Python 3.9+ and [the JSON Schema dependency](requirements.txt). |
| [Synthetic example](example-assessment.json) | A $50 qualifying health credit and a separate $100 cashable credit. The synthetic threshold is $220/month. |
| [Acceptance tests](test_check_allowance.py) | Regression cases for qualification, cash gates, waiver treatment, ICHRA, FSA, provenance and exact arithmetic. |

From this directory:

```sh
python3 -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 check_allowance.py example-assessment.json
python3 -m unittest discover -s . -p 'test_*.py'
```

The example returns a $270 monthly baseline and $220 required contribution. It reports `affordable` at the supplied threshold. Use `-` instead of a filename to read JSON from standard input. Valid assessments return JSON with exit status 0, including assessments with failing or unresolved findings. Invalid input returns exit status 2 and a diagnostic.

### Agent workflow

1. **Collect the actual terms.** Use signed terms, dated offers and effective schedules. Record each legally distinct funded amount as its own `tranches[]` item. Include cafeteria, direct, ICHRA, payroll and other amounts relevant to the subject's offer. Add the cash-gate, individual-premium and private-option sections when those features apply. Assess each offer, class or plan component separately when the evidence differs. Verify the proposed facts against elections and payroll or identify the differences. Use the `design` purpose for a controls-only review.
2. **Attach evidence to facts.** Each fact carries `value` and `evidenceIds`. An authorized reviewer establishes which sources support a fact and confirms their scope, authority, effective dates and consistency. Give the checker the records' IDs, versions, verification status and locators. Facts omitted, null, out of period, unresolved or supported by unverified evidence remain unknown. A cited conflict triggers review for an affected test. The checker validates this declaration and date coverage. Reviewers and agents verify the documents behind it.
3. **Select the purpose and basis before running.** Choose group §36B employee or related individual, group §4980H, ICHRA §36B or §4980H, or the matching Form 1095-C reporting purpose. Supply the applicable group premium or ICHRA LCSP and the newly available HRA amount. Verify the applicable vehicle, offer, premium, method and safe-harbor or reporting conditions in `methodAndOfferVerified`. Provide the evidenced monthly threshold for a threshold assessment. The engine uses those inputs and keeps dollars already included in the premium out of subsequent adjustments.
4. **Read findings and calculations together.** Each finding gives a rule ID, scope, status, fact paths, evidence IDs and authority where applicable. `pass` confirms a stated check. `fail` records a failed predicate, such as a particular election missing its cash minimum. `missing_evidence` and `review_required` explain what blocks a finding. `not_applicable` follows a verified condition or disqualification for that rule. The overall status prioritizes failed checks, then review, then missing evidence. Check numerical blockers separately. A cash-election failure has no automatic effect on the affordability calculation.
5. **Report the actual decision and its effects.** Identify what the employer funded, who can use it, what the employee pays, why any cash was available or withheld, and which evidence or decision is still needed. Retain the rule version, deliberation and source revisions for the next month or administrator. The result covers the checks run for the supplied design and purpose.

### Affordability and reporting inputs

- **Group**: Determine the applicable lowest-cost minimum-value offer and required contribution. For the §36B employee test, use self-only coverage. For the related-individual test, include the employee and relevant tax-family members offered coverage under the plan. Supply the premium net of built-in direct funding and before the separately modeled flex, waiver and other adjustments. Use zero for `otherGroupNetAdjustmentCents` only when verified. A signed adjustment can represent, for example, a verified qualifying contribution from an integrated group HRA that remains outside the input premium. [26 CFR §1.36B-2(c)(3)(v)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.36B-2)
- **ICHRA**: Verify the actual LCSP and permitted location or look-back basis, the applicable HRA amount and the number of available months. Provide a dated source for the annual amount newly available and confirm it meets the determinability rule. For example, an applicable LCSP of $500/month and $2,400 newly available over 12 months yield a $300 baseline. With $75 of separately qualifying flex, the checker shows $225 as a scenario for review. Missing evidence for that scenario does not erase a separately established ordinary baseline. The checker computes covered rules from declared facts. It relies on the plan or reviewer to determine legal class and safe-harbor applicability, confirm underlying nondiscrimination and vehicle-specific tests, select and verify benchmarks and thresholds, and apply purpose-specific decisions where the rule requires one. A sponsor or administrator handles actual participant elections, cash payment, claims, notice delivery and filing.

## Store the terms and results

Use the [input schema](assessment-input.schema.json) for a portable assessment snapshot and the [result schema](assessment-result.schema.json) for its findings. A sponsor can use JSON files, a database or another store and round-trip these two documents through the checker.

| Record | What to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Adopted allowance revision | Stable plan and revision IDs, purpose, effective dates, plan document and schedule evidence, and IDs for each distinct funded amount. Retain the original plan terms and the version used to produce each assessment snapshot. |
| Funded amount | Vehicle and purpose, policy scopes and effective maximum funding, permitted uses, cash election rights and disposition, cash eligibility gate and waiver terms if applicable, with the supporting evidence. Use stable IDs that a new administrator can map to its own systems. |
| Evidence and parameters | Immutable versions of adopted terms, offer and policy schedules, census and headcount results, payroll or elections, authorities and reviewer decisions. Include a pinpoint, authority status, controlling period and intended purpose where relevant. Use access-controlled evidence locators and avoid embedding payroll or participant details in shared design files. |
| Assessment snapshot and result | Plan, revision, subject, date, purpose, normalized input, rule version, input digest, findings, unresolved facts and numerical outputs. Record any separate approval with its scope, purpose, authority, effective period and issuer. Reassess affected determinations when the terms, offer, facts, authority or rule version changes. |

Input amounts use USD cents and their field's documented period. The threshold also accepts a rational number of cents. Calculated output amounts are signed rational cents encoded as decimal strings for the numerator and a positive denominator. For example, a $220 monthly amount is represented as `{"numerator":"22000","denominator":"1"}`. Preserve the unrounded values for comparison. Apply purpose-specific rounding when preparing a report. A null amount indicates an unresolved or inapplicable value, as explained by the result status and findings.
