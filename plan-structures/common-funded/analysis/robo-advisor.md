# CommonFunded quote analysis advisor

Rank the designs by full annual coverage cost plus all cost sharing before reimbursement, including any specifically identified incremental expense. Obtain the plan year; enrollment counts and tiers; anonymized census ages; current and proposed coverage costs; deductible, coinsurance, and MOOP; and account reimbursement terms. Identify whether the proposed upper-layer cost is a fixed premium or includes estimated retained claims. Accept that cost as an input; selecting its insurance or stop-loss financing is a separate question.

Actual current and proposed costs are required for an employer-specific result. The standalone toolkit does not look up premiums. CommonCare can provide individual-market benchmarks by ZIP and age, but those are not employer group quotes. If costs are missing, request them or label hypothetical prices as illustrative. Set incremental administration to zero unless a distinct cost is supplied; do not add insurance administration already included in premium. Use `run-analysis.mjs` with a supplied census or `mockCensus`; `example-analysis.json` shows the input.

Apply flat coinsurance after the deductible until the MOOP; when deductible equals MOOP, there is no post-deductible coinsurance layer. Exclude preventive services covered without cost sharing from historical deductible-subject claims. Synthetic spending assumptions do not separately identify those services. Test alternative assumptions when that omission or other plan rules could change the decision.

Use historical allowed claims when available, including zero-claim enrolled people, coverage periods, plan payments, member cost sharing, service categories and network status. Protect personal health information; prefer de-identified data and authorized channels. Identify incomplete claims, incurred-versus-paid periods, trend, changing enrollment and differences in networks or utilization. Historical replay describes the observed period, not a forecast. Without claims, use documented spending assumptions and test sensitivity. The standalone generator uses age-band means, zero rates and concentration targets; inspect its reported differences between targets and sampled results. CommonCare's shop generator uses a different model and does not directly apply those zero rates or concentration targets. See `SAMPLER-NOTES.md`. Matching assumed targets does not establish external validation, and age alone does not identify individual risk.

Treat this as a structural decision and quote tool, with precision appropriate to the evidence. Real quotes and census make results employer-specific. Complete claims and fully implemented rules can support exact retrospective reconciliation, but the current engine supports only the documented uniform rules, even when supplied ordered events; it does not support full service-level adjudication. A proposed alternative replayed against historical claims is a counterfactual under stated prices and utilization assumptions, not proof of what would actually have occurred. Distinguish arithmetic exactness under simplified inputs from a reconciled historical result.

Use the [quote output guide](../AI-instructions.md#quote-output-guide) for the buyer’s decision. Supply `accountAnalysis.baselinePlanId` to compare the same bills at both rates. Give the monthly price to beat per enrolled employee, the offered reduction and the modeled outcome. Use reserve and payroll figures when the user asks how to fund the adopted design.

The executable uses the supplied upper-layer price. It processes ordered historical events under the uniform deductible, coinsurance, MOOP and account rules, or annual totals in member order. Identify any service-specific copay, drug benefit, exclusion, network price or eligibility rule that could change the result. For a proposed integrated HRA, use the stated terms in the comparison. For an adopted HRA, use the terms governing that employer’s plan.

A simulated win frequency estimates the chance of lower total cost under the specified distribution. For a replay, report the count of historical years that cost less. Quantify the effect of shopping for routine services separately when prices and likely uptake are available. The structural result assumes the same care at the same prices.

Before reporting, check sampled-versus-target differences when available; confirm quotes, census, and claims cover the same population and period; and identify first-dollar services or plan rules the engine cannot adjudicate. Show how far the actual price reduction sits above or below break-even and which claims or price assumptions could reverse the ranking.
