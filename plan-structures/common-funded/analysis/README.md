# Quote analysis toolkit

This tool compares the cost of paying a defined layer of participant cost sharing through insurance premium with the cost of funding it through an account. It uses quoted plan costs and either simulated or historical claims. Real census and claims data replace assumptions with employer-specific evidence; the executable applies the simplified rules described below.

## Start here

Obtain actual prices for the current and proposed plans before making a customer-specific calculation. At minimum, gather enrolled household counts, annual quoted costs by coverage tier, deductible, MOOP and an assumed flat coinsurance rate for each plan, plus the proposed account reimbursement threshold and cap. Annualize monthly premiums. Premiums are inputs; this toolkit does not search for or invent quotes. If exact census data are unavailable, use a mock census and clearly label the result illustrative.

## Definitions and decision boundaries

- **Current cost and upper-layer cost** are full annual costs for the same enrolled population and period. A quoted premium is fixed for its contract period; an upper-layer amount that includes expected self-funded claims is an estimate, and its volatility is outside this model.
- **Released contribution** is current cost minus the proposed upper-layer cost. It is not automatically a deposited account balance or a guaranteed saving.
- **Reimbursement** is the eligible claim paid by the account under the modeled threshold and cap. The current implementation approximates eligibility using aggregate cost sharing, not individual expense categories or dates.
- **Account-layer gain** is released contribution minus incremental reimbursements relative to the current account and any specifically supplied incremental administration. It can be negative. It is distinct from unused account budget and from combined savings.
- **Participant residual** is modeled cost sharing after account reimbursement. Combined cost change includes its difference between designs, so shifting costs between employer and participant is not counted as a saving.
- **Account budget** is a population-level planning reserve. The tool does not limit promised reimbursements to that reserve. Expected unused budget is the average of positive unspent amounts; it does not offset years in which claims exceed the reserve and is not net experience gain.
- **Simulation outcomes** are conditional on supplied or assumed distributions. Percentiles and shortfall frequencies describe the generated scenarios; they are not validated forecasts or guarantees. Observed dominance means only that one design wins in the sampled scenarios, not all possible states.

The quoted premium is a contractual expense that purchases coverage. Account reimbursements depend on claims and the adopted terms. The tool compares their modeled costs; it does not forecast renewal prices, network changes, medical outcomes, or upper-layer claim volatility.

For a one-command demonstration, run `node run-analysis.mjs example-analysis.json` from this directory. Replace its hypothetical prices and plan terms with actual quotes before interpreting the result. The example generates 50 single-coverage employees averaging age 40; `mockCensus` can also specify `minAge`, `maxAge`, `familyFraction`, `dependentsPerFamily`, and `seed`. Alternatively supply `census` directly; [sample-census.json](sample-census.json) shows the format. The synthetic family model assumes the first dependent is an adult and additional dependents are children; it does not infer real enrollment tiers.

`annualFixed` is the full annual cost per enrolled single household, not an employee payroll contribution. `family.annualFixed` is a single combined family rate; when quoted tiers have different prices, this simplified runner cannot accurately represent them yet. `coinsurance` is a decimal such as `0.2`, applied after the deductible until the MOOP. When deductible equals MOOP, no post-deductible coinsurance is incurred in this simplified model.

The model assumes input bills are deductible-subject allowed expenses. Preventive care covered without cost sharing should be excluded from historical inputs. Synthetic age-band means are broad assumptions and do not separately identify preventive claims: treat the resulting reimbursement estimates as directional and use sensitivity analysis if preventive amounts or other first-dollar services are material. Special copays, service-specific deductibles, network and pricing differences may alter results; interpret a narrow result accordingly.

No additional administration is assumed: `incrementalAdministration` defaults to zero. Enter a nonzero amount only when a specific incremental cost is known. Existing insurance administrative expense is already reflected in the quoted premium and must not be added again.

The toolkit separates scenario generation from comparison. It requires Node.js and no dependencies. `run-simulation.mjs input.json` emits seeded annual allowed bills for each census member. Input contains `census: [{id, age, householdId}]`, optional `years`, `seed`, `ageBands: [{maxAge, zeroProbability, annualMean}]`, and `concentration: [{top, share}]`. Defaults reproduce the age and concentration assumptions in CommonCare's major-medical cost model. The output reports target and actual concentration, age means, zero rates, and residuals. Inspect them before using results; finite samples or conflicting assumptions can prevent a fit.

### Evidence and precision

| Evidence | What the result means |
|---|---|
| Mock census and simulated claims | A reproducible illustration under stated assumptions |
| Actual quotes and census with simulated claims | An employer-specific structural quote estimate, conditional on the simulated claims distribution |
| Actual historical annual allowed totals | A reproducible replay of those totals under this tool's simplified rules; not necessarily the historical plan's actual adjudication |
| Complete dated claims, actual prices and eligibility, and fully implemented plan and account rules | An exact retrospective calculation can be established by reconciling against actual payments. This requires a more detailed adjudication engine than the current executable. |

Replaying a proposed alternative against past claims is a counterfactual, even if every supplied claim is processed exactly. Different networks, prices, coverage, and participant behavior might have produced different care and claims. Historical reconciliation and counterfactual replay do not guarantee future costs.

Calibration is performed over all simulated person-years, preserving age-band averages and approximate zero rates while fitting ranked population spending shares. It does not force each simulated year to have the same total or concentration. These are assumed targets, not proof of empirical validation. Person-years start independently, but the calibration couples the resulting draws; persistent conditions and family correlations are not modeled. CommonCare's production simulator uses a separate sampling method and adjusts concentration when an age band's zero rate conflicts with the targets.

`run-comparison.mjs input.json` accepts `scenarios: [{id, bills: [{id, householdId, allowed}]}]` and `plans: [{id, annualFixed, deductible, coinsurance, moop, hraThreshold, hraCap, family}]`. Amounts are annual dollars; coinsurance is a decimal. Each household ID groups covered members; omit it for a single-person household. For multi-person households, every plan needs `family: {annualFixed, deductible, deductibleType, moop, hraThreshold, hraCap}`, with `deductibleType` either `embedded` or `aggregate`. Family premium and HRA cap apply once per household. The individual MOOP applies to each member and the family MOOP to the household. Every scenario must include every enrolled person, including zero claims. Historical scenarios may be supplied directly. Results report population-year averages.

The model assumes identical in-network allowed bills and coverage across plans, one deductible and coinsurance rate, and a cost-sharing HRA above its threshold. Family annual totals are processed in supplied member order, not actual claim chronology; this approximation may affect allocation and aggregate-deductible results. It does not model multiple claim types, pricing differences, utilization changes, stop-loss or level funding. See [robo-advisor.md](robo-advisor.md) before interpreting results.

For the central CommonFunded calculation, add `accountAnalysis: {baselinePlanId: "current"}`. Optionally include `annualAccountBudget` as the total population-level planning reserve; it is not a limit on promised reimbursements. Each plan's `annualFixed` is the supplied annual cost per enrolled single household; `family.annualFixed` is the corresponding family amount. These may be fixed premiums or allocated estimates for an upper layer, provided the same population and period are used. The output's `accountLayer` reports the released contribution, incremental HRA reimbursements, account gain, percentiles, shortfall probability, and participant residual change. `expectedCombinedCostChange` includes that residual change; negative favors the proposed design. If upper-layer costs are estimates, these outputs do not simulate their volatility. A reimbursement cap is a liability limit, not necessarily cash deposited into an account.

Percentiles and shortfall probabilities are conditional on the synthetic distribution and pooling method, not precise forecasts. The output flags whether concentration targets are within one percentage point; this is a diagnostic threshold, not actuarial validation. If it fails, report the discrepancy and investigate before relying on the result. Compare multiple seeds and plausible age means, concentration, and benefit assumptions to see whether the direction is stable. Report the result as a measured structural comparison, not a guaranteed budget.

Run a small worked input with `node run-comparison.mjs example-comparison.json` from this directory. It is a hypothetical two-scenario illustration, not a population forecast. Run the calculation check with `node --test comparison.test.mjs`. For synthetic input, run `node run-simulation.mjs census.json` and copy its `scenarios` array into the comparison input alongside the quoted plans and `accountAnalysis` settings. Use actual annual allowed-claim scenarios directly when available.
