# Working with CommonFunded

CommonFunded separates two jobs that are often bundled into a health plan: paying for routine care and financing large medical claims. It uses CommonFunds (a concept wrapper for all legal account-based health plans) to reimburse eligible expenses in a defined account-funded layer, while insurance or another arrangement addresses claims in the separate upper layer. The aim is to find out whether this division reduces the total cost of coverage and care for a particular population, and by how much.

Explain the actual mechanism: a higher-deductible plan may cost less; the resulting difference can fund reimbursements for the additional participant cost sharing. Premium is paid regardless of claims, while an account reimbursement is paid when an eligible expense occurs. Whether the exchange is favorable depends on the prices, benefits and claims—not on the deductible alone. Unused reimbursement capacity is not automatically a saving or a cash deposit.

The central distinction is which costs to pool and which to fund through accounts. That decision is separate from whether the employer insures or self-funds the upper layer. An account-funded layer can make routine expenses visible and leave unused reimbursement capacity with the employer under the plan terms, while the selected upper-layer arrangement addresses larger claims.

## Analyze the opportunity

When the user wants to explore but has not supplied a scenario, give a brief explanation and make it concrete. The explorer returns `exampleResult`, calculated by CommonCare from the hypothetical `accountExample`; explain its account gain or shortfall and participant cost change. You can use this returned result even if you cannot send POST requests. If it is unavailable, say so and do not invent results. When asked how to get an analysis, explain what you can do for the user and ask for the minimum missing facts; don't lead with instructions to clone a repository or write JSON. Use available tools yourself and adapt inputs. If a user-specific analysis requires a request you cannot make, say so and offer the public script or a ready-to-run request.

1. **Gather the inputs.** Ask for the plan year; covered households, tiers and ages; current and proposed coverage costs; deductibles, coinsurance and out-of-pocket maximums; and current and proposed account reimbursement terms. Use actual quotes and census data when available. Label hypothetical inputs and individual-market benchmark prices clearly; benchmarks are not employer group quotes.
2. **Measure the cost released.** Compare annual costs for the same population and period. Current cost minus proposed upper-layer cost is the released contribution. Accept the upper-layer cost as supplied; choosing insurance, level funding, self-funding or stop-loss for that layer is a separate decision. Identify whether the amount is a fixed premium or includes estimated retained claims.
3. **Compare reimbursements.** Run the same medical scenarios through both designs. Subtract the additional account reimbursements and any specifically identified incremental cost from the released contribution. Show the expected gain or shortfall and its range across scenarios. Assume no added administration cost unless a distinct cost is supplied.
4. **Account for participant costs.** Report the change in cost sharing left with participants after reimbursement, and include it in the combined economic result. Moving a cost between the employer and participants does not itself create savings.
5. **Make the decision clear.** Compare total costs across the relevant claims distribution rather than applying the plan to an average bill. If one plan costs less across the full relevant range, explain that dominance; a win in sampled scenarios alone does not establish it. State which option has the lower modeled total cost, the size of the difference, and the material benefit or network differences that could change the choice. Test assumptions that could reverse the result. If the quoted economics do not support the structure, say so.

For example, a $500,000 current plan and a $350,000 proposed upper-layer arrangement release $150,000. If the proposed account pays $100,000 more than the current account, the account-layer gain is $50,000 before any specified incremental costs. The combined saving is also $50,000 only if participant residual costs do not change. If the upper-layer cost includes estimated retained claims, actual combined experience also depends on those claims.

Use [analysis/README.md](analysis/README.md) for the input formats, commands, calculation rules and limits. Historical claims can strengthen the analysis: distinguish a replay under the implemented rules from reconciliation with a real plan's payments, and explain that a proposed alternative assumes the observed care and prices would have remained the same.

## Explain implementation

Read [human-readable.md](human-readable.md) for the design, economics and implementation choices. Distinguish the participant's deductible and out-of-pocket maximum, the account's reimbursement threshold and cap, and any employer stop-loss attachment point. An account cap does not cap a self-funded employer's separate exposure to major-medical claims.

An integrated group-plan HRA and an excepted-benefit HRA are different arrangements with different eligibility and limits. Apply the relevant plan terms and integration rules to the facts; do not assume every employer or participant uses the same structure. Explain how participant elections, network access, reimbursement eligibility and actual plan documents affect the result. Use the substantive guide and cited authorities for those details.

The [CommonDocs repository](https://github.com/commoncare-dev/commondocs/tree/main/plan-structures/common-funded) is open for independent use and critique. An agent with shell access can clone it, enter `plan-structures/common-funded/analysis`, and run `node run-analysis.mjs example-analysis.json` without a CommonCare account. Those scripts are generated from the maintained CommonCare TypeScript functions; their executable source is available in the repository. The standalone and GPS spending generators have different assumptions. Treat `human-readable.md` as the substantive source; generated vector-store files are retrieval aids. Do not invent quotes, plan terms or claims, and distinguish a proposed design from an employer's adopted arrangement.
